Every syllable in Mandarin has a tone: the shape the pitch of your voice makes as you say it. Change the tone and
you change the word. 妈 (mā) is "mother" and 马 (mǎ) is "horse".

## The shapes

- **First tone (ā)** is high and flat, like holding a note.
- **Second tone (á)** rises, like asking "what?".
- **Third tone (ǎ)** dips low and comes back up a little.
- **Fourth tone (à)** falls sharply, like a firm "no!".

There is also a **neutral tone**, short and light, with no mark at all, as in 吗 (ma).

## Two rules that change tones

Two third tones in a row are hard to say, so the first one rises: 你好 is written nǐ hǎo but said ní hǎo. And 不
(bù, "not") becomes bú before a fourth tone, as in 不客气 (bú kèqi, "you're welcome").

## Practise with your hands

Draw the shape of each tone in the air as you say it. It feels silly, and it works. Try lesson 1 in the free
practice lessons, then send your tutor a voice note of all four.
